Methodology & Sources

UK projections apply each plan's income-contingent formula — 9% (or 6% for Plan 9) of salary above the relevant threshold, with the balance written off after the plan's term (25, 30, or 40 years). USA Standard uses the amortising annuity formula: M = P × r(1+r)^n / ((1+r)^n − 1). PSLF caps the payment schedule at 120 months and forgives the remainder. Graduated and Extended plans are approximated as Standard in v1.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the UK student loan threshold for Plan 2?
For 2026, Plan 2 borrowers repay 9% of every pound earned above £28,470 per year. Anything below the threshold means zero repayment that month. Any balance left after 30 years from the first April after course completion is written off.
What is Plan 5 and who is on it?
Plan 5 is for English students starting university from 1 August 2023. The threshold is £25,000 (lower than Plan 2), the rate is still 9%, but the write-off period is extended to 40 years. Most Plan 5 borrowers will pay back significantly more total interest than Plan 2 borrowers.
How does PSLF (Public Service Loan Forgiveness) work?
PSLF cancels the remaining balance on US Federal Direct loans after you make 120 qualifying monthly payments while working full-time for a qualifying public-service employer. The forgiven amount is not taxed federally. This calculator caps the schedule at 120 payments and treats the remainder as forgiven.
Should I make extra student loan payments?
USA Federal: yes if your interest rate exceeds your expected investment return, and only after maxing employer 401(k) match and any high-interest credit card debt. UK income-contingent: rarely worth it — extra payments only help if you would otherwise pay back the full loan before write-off, which only happens for high earners on Plan 1, 2, or 4.
What's the difference between UK and USA student loans?
UK student loans are income-contingent (you only pay above a salary threshold, and the balance is written off after 25 to 40 years) — they behave more like a graduate tax. USA Federal Direct loans are amortising — you pay a fixed schedule until paid off, with PSLF as the only mainstream forgiveness route.
Which UK Plan am I on if I don't know?
Look at the year and country of your first course. England + Northern Ireland: Plan 1 (started before 2012), Plan 2 (2012–July 2023), Plan 5 (from August 2023). Scotland: Plan 4. Wales: Plan 2 or Plan 5 depending on year. Postgraduate Master's/PhD: Plan 9 (formerly PGL). Log in to studentloanrepayment.co.uk to confirm — the wrong plan gives the wrong threshold and write-off rules.
What's the most common student-loan mistake?
UK borrowers overpaying when they shouldn't. If you're unlikely to clear the balance before write-off (most Plan 2/5 borrowers don't), every extra pound is wasted. Run your salary trajectory through the calculator first — if it shows substantial write-off, redirect the overpayment to a pension or ISA instead. USA borrowers' most common mistake is missing PSLF certification deadlines, which can void years of qualifying payments.
What if my salary stays below the UK threshold for many years?
You pay zero each month, interest still accrues on the balance, and the entire balance is eventually written off at the 25/30/40-year mark (depending on Plan). For low-earning careers (typically below the threshold by year 5), the write-off is highly likely — making any extra payments unwise. The calculator caps total payments at 0 if your salary trajectory never breaches the threshold.
I've seen my projected total — what should I do?
USA: if total payments well exceed the principal, look into Income-Driven Repayment (IDR) plans (PAYE, SAVE) which cap monthly payment at 5–10% of discretionary income and forgive after 20–25 years. UK Plan 2/5: confirm whether you'll clear the loan before write-off — if not, stop any overpayments. UK Plan 4 (Scotland) and Plan 1: lower thresholds + 25-year write-off mean overpayment can save real money for high earners.
Student loan vs other debt — which to pay off first?
Hierarchy: credit cards (20%+ APR) → personal loans (8–14%) → mortgage (4–7%) → USA Federal student loans (4–8%). UK income-contingent loans should usually NOT be prioritised — they behave like a 9% marginal tax above the threshold, not a normal loan. Capture employer 401(k)/pension match before any extra debt payments — it's a guaranteed 50–100% return that beats every debt's interest rate.
