An ISA gives you tax-free growth and tax-free withdrawals, with full access at any age. A pension gives you upfront tax relief on your contributions but locks the money up until age 55 (rising to 57 from April 2028). For most UK savers the answer is not “one or the other” — it is the right mix.

ISA vs pension: at-a-glance

Feature Stocks & Shares ISA Pension (SIPP / Workplace)
Tax on the way in After-tax (no relief) Pre-tax (20% basic-rate relief automatic; higher/additional via Self Assessment)
Tax on growth None None
Tax on the way out None 25% tax-free lump sum; balance taxed as income
2026/27 annual allowance £20,000 £60,000 (or 100% of relevant earnings if lower)

Employer contributions No Yes — auto-enrolment minimum 3% from employer
Inheritance tax treatment In your estate Generally outside your estate (defined-contribution pensions)

What to model

Compare the after-tax value of contributing £500/month into an ISA vs an equivalent gross-of-tax-relief pension contribution over 25 years. Use a 6% real return. The pension will look larger pre-withdrawal; remember to apply your expected retirement marginal rate to 75% of the pot before declaring a winner.

The order most planners recommend

A common decision order is: 1. contribute enough to your workplace pension to capture the full employer match; 2. fill the ISA for flexibility and tax-free withdrawals; 3. top up the pension for additional tax relief, especially in higher-rate years. The exact split depends on your bracket, time horizon, and access needs — but starting with the free employer money is almost never wrong.
