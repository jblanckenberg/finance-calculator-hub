A Roth IRA takes after-tax dollars in and pays you tax-free withdrawals in retirement. A Traditional IRA takes pre-tax dollars in and taxes you on the way out. The right account depends almost entirely on whether your tax bracket today is higher or lower than the one you expect in retirement.

What to model

Common mistakes

OPERATOR_TO_FILL — ~200w: Cover the most common mistakes: contributing to a Roth above the income limit (and not recharacterising), forgetting that pro-rata rules apply if you have any pre-tax IRA money when doing a backdoor Roth, treating the $7,000 limit as a per-account limit rather than a combined limit across all IRAs, and ignoring the five-year clock on Roth conversions.

The decision rule

Pick the Roth if you expect your retirement tax bracket to be equal to or higher than today’s. Pick the Traditional if you expect it to be meaningfully lower. When unsure, split the difference: many savers contribute to both throughout their career to hedge bracket risk and keep their withdrawal mix flexible.
