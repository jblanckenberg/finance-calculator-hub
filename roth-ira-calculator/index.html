Methodology & Sources

This calculator applies the 2026 IRS Roth IRA contribution limits ($7,000 base, $8,000 with the age-50 catch-up) and the MAGI phase-out windows for single, married-filing-jointly, and married-filing-separately filers. Growth is compounded annually using a half-year contribution convention. The "Tax Saved vs Taxable Account" figure compares the Roth balance against an equivalent taxable brokerage account assuming a 22% effective drag on annual returns.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Roth IRA contribution limit for 2026?
$7,000 if you are under 50, or $8,000 if you are 50 or older. Both numbers can be reduced or eliminated based on your modified adjusted gross income (MAGI) and filing status.
At what income am I phased out of a Roth IRA?
For 2026, single filers phase out between $150,000 and $165,000 of MAGI. Married filing jointly phases out between $236,000 and $246,000. Married filing separately while living with your spouse phases out between $0 and $10,000.
Can I contribute to a Roth IRA if I have a 401(k)?
Yes. The 401(k) and Roth IRA limits are separate. You can max both — $23,500 to your 401(k) plus $7,000 to your Roth IRA in 2026, subject to the MAGI phase-out for the Roth.
What is a backdoor Roth IRA?
If your MAGI exceeds the Roth phase-out, you can contribute to a Traditional IRA (no income limit) and then convert it to a Roth. The conversion is taxable to the extent of pre-tax dollars in the IRA. This calculator does not model conversions — it shows direct contribution growth only.
Are Roth IRA withdrawals really tax-free?
Qualified withdrawals are tax-free and penalty-free if the account is at least 5 years old AND you are at least 59½. Contributions (not earnings) can be withdrawn anytime without tax or penalty.
USA-only — what's the closest UK/SA equivalent?
Roth IRA is a USA-only account. Closest equivalents by tax treatment: UK — Stocks & Shares ISA (£20,000/yr, contributions from after-tax income, all gains and withdrawals tax-free at any age). South Africa — TFSA (R36,000/yr, R500,000 lifetime, same after-tax-in, tax-free-out structure). The Roth IRA's main differentiator is the age 59½ qualified-withdrawal rule; ISAs and TFSAs allow withdrawals at any age without penalty.
What's the most common Roth IRA mistake?
Contributing while above the MAGI phase-out limit. The penalty is a 6% excise tax per year on the excess until corrected — and many high earners only discover it at tax-filing time the following spring. The fix is to withdraw the excess (and any growth on it) before the tax deadline, or recharacterise to a traditional IRA. Always check your MAGI before contributing if your income is anywhere near $150k single or $236k MFJ.
What if I'm phased out — is there a workaround?
Yes, the backdoor Roth IRA. You contribute to a non-deductible Traditional IRA (no income limit), then immediately convert it to a Roth IRA. The conversion is taxable only on any earnings between contribution and conversion (usually negligible if done quickly) and on any pre-tax IRA balance you already hold (the pro-rata rule). Mega backdoor Roth via after-tax 401(k) contributions allows even larger amounts if your employer's plan supports it.
I've contributed — what's the next step?
Pick your investments. A common low-cost choice is a target-date retirement fund matching your retirement year, or a 3-fund portfolio (US total market + international + bond index). Don't leave Roth IRA contributions sitting in cash — the tax-free growth benefit only matters if the money is actually invested. Recheck contribution limits every year — the IRS adjusts them for inflation.
Roth IRA vs Traditional IRA — which wins?
Roth wins if your marginal tax rate at retirement is HIGHER than now (typical for younger savers expecting career growth). Traditional wins if your marginal tax rate now is HIGHER than at retirement (typical for peak-earnings 50-somethings). A diversification approach: contribute to both across your career so retirement withdrawals can be tax-optimised by drawing from whichever bucket has the better treatment that year.
