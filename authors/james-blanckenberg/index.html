About

I'm James — the person behind FinCalcHub. I started the site in 2024 after spending years running the same compound-interest, retirement, and take-home-pay numbers on a notepad and finding no single tool that handled all three regions I care about (the USA, the UK, and South Africa) without paywalls, sign-up forms, or pre-calculation ads.

I'm a sole operator, not a venture-backed startup. I write the calculators, the explanations, and the underlying formulas; I also run a sister site for small-business owners called BusCalcTools. My background is in product and technology, not regulated financial advice — which is exactly why this site sticks to running the maths, not selling products. I've personally managed my own money across three tax regimes for the better part of two decades, which is where most of the cross-border content draws from.

Editorial principles

Four rules shape every calculator and article on FinCalcHub. Real numbers — every calc runs the actual formula a lender, payroll system, or pension administrator would use, against the current published bands. Primary sources only — HMRC, SARS, the IRS, the Bank of England, the ONS. We link to the source so you can verify it yourself. No commission take — we don't accept affiliate fees from product issuers; ads on the site are display ads served by Google AdSense, never product placements. Plain language — if a sentence needs a glossary entry, the glossary entry comes too. Full detail lives in the editorial policy.

Credentials & disclosure

FinCalcHub is independent and educational. The calculators and articles here help you do the maths; they are not personal financial, tax, or investment advice, and they're not a substitute for a chartered planner, a registered tax practitioner, or a regulated adviser. Where you need formal advice — particularly on pensions, complex tax positions, or estate planning — please talk to a qualified professional in your jurisdiction. See our editorial policy, advertising & affiliate disclosure, and the About page for the full picture.
